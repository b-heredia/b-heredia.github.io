01 — Web Application

TrustLens: a link checker for everyday people

The problem

Who it helps

Many people, especially those who aren’t familiar with cybersecurity threats, can click a malicious link that looks legitimate. I built TrustLens to help people spot warning signs in a URL before they click.

What I built

Front end, back end, logic and tests

I built TrustLens myself, end to end:

  • Back end: a Python and Flask web app that checks each link.
  • Logic: six predefined checks, including HTTPS, IP addresses, long URLs, hidden usernames, URL shorteners and unusual domain endings.
  • Front end: HTML and CSS, with a plain-language explanation for every warning.
  • Testing: 13 automated tests with Python’s unittest.

TrustLens doesn’t guarantee whether a site is safe. It gives people information so they can make a more informed choice.

Debugging

When the results didn’t match what I expected

While building TrustLens, some results didn’t match what I expected. I reviewed my analyzer logic, tested functions one at a time and adjusted until the results were right.

One big lesson was the difference between testing a single function and testing the whole application. I’m now more comfortable troubleshooting and working through problems step by step.

02 — Geek Week

SQL, Excel and timed challenges

SQL

From memorizing to understanding

At first, SQL was hard for me, especially remembering query structure and how commands work together. With repetition, I learned to break queries into smaller steps and understand the logic behind each one.

Excel and hot keys

Attention to detail under time pressure

In Excel, I practiced formulas, functions, cell references and working with data, along with keyboard shortcuts and timed exercises. It sharpened my attention to detail and made me more comfortable fixing my own mistakes.

I learned that technical growth takes practice, patience and understanding why something works, not just memorizing commands.

Typing

About 86 words per minute

I worked on my typing speed and accuracy throughout the program and now type about 86 words per minute, more than double the 40 WPM benchmark. It helps me write code, take notes and communicate with my team faster.
