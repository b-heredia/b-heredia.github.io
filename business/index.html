01 — Client Project

Medline: from repeat incidents to root causes

The problem

What Medline needed

Medline wanted one central web platform to standardize how warehouse incidents are captured and reported.

The bigger issue was that the same warehouse problems kept being reported one at a time, without anyone recognizing, tracking and permanently fixing the underlying cause.

Medline wanted to use AI to spot recurring patterns across incidents, register them as owned Problem records, and route them to the right IT teams until they were resolved. The goal: fewer repeat incidents, by fixing root causes instead of reacting to the same issue again and again.

Our approach

Connecting incidents, patterns and owners

We looked at how one platform could connect incident reporting, AI pattern recognition, problem ownership and IT resolution. We mapped what information the platform would capture and how the records would relate to each other.

Our draft data model:

  • Owners: the people or teams responsible
  • Incidents: each reported issue
  • Problems: recurring patterns made into owned records
  • Problem History: every change until it’s resolved
  • Dismissed Patterns: patterns reviewed and set aside

This helped us think through how incidents link to recurring problems, how ownership gets set, and how a problem’s history is tracked.

Our proposal

One platform, from report to resolution

In our proposal, we recommended a single web platform that would:

  1. Capture warehouse incidents through a standard reporting process.
  2. Use AI to identify recurring incident patterns.
  3. Create Problem records from those patterns.
  4. Assign each problem an owner.
  5. Route problems to the right IT teams.
  6. Track problem history through resolution.
  7. Keep resolved issues from being reported again as new incidents.

The vision was to move from reacting to incidents to finding and fixing the problems behind them.

My role

UX Lead

I served as UX Lead for Relanta, contributing to our shared project work and helping move the team’s responsibilities forward.

The project pushed me to think beyond individual technical tasks and ask how a technology solution actually solves a business problem. I learned to understand the client’s needs before deciding on a technical approach.

Looking back

What went well and what I’d change

I learned how to approach a real business problem and how data structures, application design, AI and business processes work together in one solution.

Next time, I’d set clear team roles, accountability and communication expectations earlier. I’d also spend more time checking each part of our solution against the client’s requirements.

02 — Consulting Company

What Relanta taught me

Teamwork

More than dividing up assignments

Working as a consulting team taught me that teamwork takes communication, accountability, trust and consistent participation from everyone.

I learned to take initiative and stay committed to moving work forward, and also how much shared responsibility and clear expectations matter.

Feedback

Feedback as a way to improve

I learned to see feedback as a chance to improve the work, not as criticism of my abilities.

It also showed me how important it is to communicate changes, adjust quickly and keep the team aligned with what the client expects.

Professionalism

What professionalism means to me now

Professionalism means being dependable, communicating clearly, meeting commitments, accepting feedback and respecting other people’s contributions.

It also means remembering that good technology starts with understanding the people and business problems it’s meant to serve.
