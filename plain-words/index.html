A glossary of AI, code, UX and design words.

I'm learning as I go, and these are the words I've had to look up. Each one is in plain language with an everyday example. If it helps me remember, maybe it helps you learn too.

A/B testing

DesignSplit testing

Showing two versions of a design to different groups of people to see which one works better.

Think of it likeBaking two batches of cookies with different recipes and seeing which one disappears first.
In real techA site shows half its visitors a green Sign up button and half a blue one, then compares how many people sign up.

Related: Usability testing, Quantitative user research

Above the fold

DesignBelow the fold

The part of a page you see before scrolling is above the fold. Anything you have to scroll to see is below it.

Think of it likeThe top half of a folded newspaper on a newsstand, the part people see first.
In real techDesigners put the most important message and main button above the fold, like a headline and a Contact button.

Related: Responsive design, Visual design

Accessibility

Designa11y

Designing so people with disabilities can use a product, including people who are blind, deaf, color-blind or can't use a mouse.

Think of it likeA ramp next to the stairs. It helps wheelchair users, and also people with strollers or suitcases.
In real techAlt text on images, strong color contrast, captions on videos and a site you can use with only a keyboard.

Related: Inclusive design, Ethical design

Adobe XD

Tools

Adobe's app for designing and prototyping websites and apps.

Think of it likeA digital sketchbook made for screens.
In real techAdobe stopped adding new features to XD in 2023, so many teams have moved to Figma.

Related: Figma, Sketch, Prototype

Affinity map

DesignAffinity diagram

A way to sort lots of research notes into groups so patterns show up.

Think of it likeDumping out a junk drawer and sorting things into piles: batteries, pens, keys.
In real techAfter user interviews, a team writes each finding on a sticky note and clusters similar notes together.

Related: User research, Brainstorming

Affordance

Design

A clue in a design that shows how something can be used.

Think of it likeA door handle you pull and a flat plate you push. The shape tells you what to do.

Related: Intuitive design, UI elements

Agile

Process

A way of working where a team builds in small pieces, gets feedback often and adjusts as they go.

Think of it likeCooking a new dish by tasting and adjusting as you go, instead of following the recipe once and hoping.
In real techTeams work in short cycles called sprints, usually 1 to 4 weeks long, and meet briefly each day to check in.

Related: Waterfall, Agile UX, User stories

Agile UX

Process

Fitting UX design into an Agile team, so research and design happen in small steps alongside the developers.

Think of it likeA chef and a server working together on every course, instead of handing over a finished menu.
In real techA designer tests one screen with users during a sprint, so developers can build the improved version in the next one.

Related: Agile, Lean UX, Design sprint

AI agent

AI

Think of it likeAn assistant you give a task to, who can open files and look things up until it's done.
In real techClaude Code can read files, run commands and make changes to finish a coding task.

Animation design

DesignMotion design

Using motion in an interface to guide attention, give feedback or make changes feel smooth.

Think of it likeA turn signal on a car. The blinking tells others what's about to happen.
In real techA heart that pops when you like a post, or a menu that slides in so you can see where it came from.

Related: Interaction design, Visual design

API

CodeApplication Programming Interface

A set of rules that lets one app ask another app for information or to do something.

Think of it likeA waiter. You tell the waiter what you want, they tell the kitchen, and they bring back your food. You never go into the kitchen yourself.
In real techA weather app asks a weather service's API for today's forecast and shows you the answer.

Related: Flask, JavaScript

Card sorting

Design

A research method where people group topics into categories that make sense to them.

Think of it likeAsking friends to organize your kitchen and seeing where they'd look for the spoons.

Case study

Design

A story about a project that shows the problem, your process, the decisions you made and the results.

Think of it likeA recipe card plus the story of how you perfected the dish.
In real techMost UX portfolios are built from 2 to 4 case studies. Hiring managers want to see how you think, not just the final screens.

Related: Portfolio, Design deliverables

Claude

AIClaude models

Anthropic's family of AI models. They come in four sizes, from most powerful to fastest: Fable, Opus, Sonnet and Haiku.

Think of it likeCoffee sizes. A bigger one takes longer and costs more, a small one is quick and cheap, and you pick what fits the moment.
In real techAs of October 2026, the newest versions are Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5. The version numbers go up as new models come out, but the four names stay the same.

Related: Claude Fable, Claude Opus, Claude Sonnet, Claude Haiku, Large language model

Claude Fable

AI

Anthropic's most capable Claude model, made for the hardest reasoning and for long, multi-step agent work. It's also the slowest and most expensive.

Think of it likeCalling in the top expert for the toughest case.
In real techGood for complex research, tricky problems and agents that keep working on one task for a long time. Newest version as of October 2026: Fable 5.1.

Related: Claude, Claude Opus, AI agent

Claude Haiku

AI

The smallest, fastest and cheapest Claude model.

Think of it likeA quick text instead of a long phone call.
In real techGood for simple tasks done in huge numbers, like sorting messages, pulling details out of text or deciding where to send a request. Newest version as of October 2026: Haiku 5.5. Haiku 4.5 is an older version.

Related: Claude, Claude Sonnet, Token

Claude Opus

AI

A very capable Claude model that balances deep thinking with good speed. Anthropic suggests starting with Opus if you aren't sure which model to pick.

Think of it likeAn experienced team lead who can handle almost anything.
In real techGood for long coding projects, writing, analysis and agents. Newest version as of October 2026: Opus 5.5.

Related: Claude, Claude Fable, Claude Sonnet

Claude Sonnet

AI

The middle Claude model, with Anthropic's best mix of speed and intelligence.

Think of it likeA reliable everyday car: quick, smart and not too pricey.
In real techGood for everyday chat, writing, coding help and apps that need answers that are both fast and strong. Newest version as of October 2026: Sonnet 5.5.

Related: Claude, Claude Opus, Claude Haiku

Clickstream analysis

Design

Studying the path of clicks people take through a website.

Think of it likeFollowing footprints in the snow to see where people walked.
In real techAnalytics might show most visitors leave on the checkout page, which tells the team where to look.

Related: Quantitative user research, User flow

Closing tag

CodeEnd tag

The second half of a tag pair. It has a slash before the name and tells the browser where an element ends.

Think of it likeThe closing quotation mark at the end of a quote. Without it, you can't tell where the quote stops.
In real tech</p> closes a paragraph. A few tags, like <img> and <br>, don't need one. Forgetting a closing tag can make the rest of the page look wrong.

Related: Tag, Element, Nesting

Cognitive load

Design

How much mental effort it takes to use something. Less is usually better.

Think of it likeTrying to remember a long phone number versus a short one.
In real techBreaking a long form into short steps lowers cognitive load.

Related: Hick's law, Usability

Comment

CodeCode comment

A note in the code that the browser ignores. It's there for people reading the code, not for visitors to the page.

Think of it likeA sticky note in the margin of a book. It helps the reader, but it isn't part of the story.
In real techIn HTML a comment looks like <!-- main menu -->. CSS uses /* note */ and JavaScript uses // note. Anyone can see comments by viewing the page source, so never put passwords in them.

Related: HTML, CSS, JavaScript

Commit

Tools

A saved snapshot of changes to a project, with a short message describing what changed.

Think of it likeSaving a checkpoint in a video game, so you can always go back to that moment.
In real techAdding a project to my portfolio was one commit: Add Agents with Guardrails project page.

Related: Git, Repository

Competitor analysis

Design

Looking closely at similar products to learn what they do well and where there's room to be better.

Think of it likeChecking out the other food trucks on the block before opening your own.
In real techA designer compares the sign-up flow of five apps and notes what's easy and what's frustrating.

Related: UX strategy, UX audit

Design

Making similar things look and work the same way across a product.

Think of it likeEvery stop sign is red and the same shape, so you never have to think about it.
In real techEvery main button uses the same color, and every page has the menu in the same spot.

Related: Design system, Style guide

Contextual inquiry

DesignContextual enquiry

Watching and talking with people while they do a task in their real environment.

Think of it likeLearning a family recipe by cooking it in grandma's kitchen instead of asking her over the phone.
In real techA researcher sits with a nurse during a shift to see how she really uses the hospital software.

CSS

CodeCascading Style Sheets

The language that styles a web page: colors, fonts, spacing and layout.

Think of it likeThe paint, furniture and decorations in a house.
In real techh1 { color: black; } makes every main heading black. CSS is often saved in its own file, usually called style.css, and connected to the page with <link rel="stylesheet" href="style.css">.

Design

Tag

CodeHTML tag

A label in angle brackets that tells the browser what a piece of content is. Most tags come in pairs: one to open and one to close.

Think of it likeLabels on storage boxes: "Photos", "Books". The label tells you what is inside before you open it.
In real tech<p>Hello</p> marks "Hello" as a paragraph. <p> opens it and </p> closes it.
Common tags, grouped by job
TagWhat it doesExample
Page structure
<!DOCTYPE html>The very first line. Tells the browser this is a modern HTML page. Not really a tag, more of an instruction.<!DOCTYPE html>
<html>...</html>Wraps the whole page. Everything else goes inside it.<html lang="en">...</html>
<head>...</head>Behind-the-scenes info about the page. Nothing in here shows on the page itself.<head><title>...</title></head>
<meta>Extra details for browsers and search engines, like the character set or page description. No closing tag.<meta charset="utf-8">
<title>...</title>The name shown in the browser tab.<title>Plain Words</title>
<body>...</body>Everything people actually see on the page.<body><h1>Hi</h1></body>
Text
<h1>...</h1> to <h6>Headings, from biggest to smallest. h1 is the largest and most important (the main title), h2 is the next size down, then h3, h4 and h5, and h6 is the smallest. Think of an outline: h1 is the book title, h2 the chapters, h3 the sections inside them. CSS can change the sizes, but the order of importance stays the same.<h2>My Projects</h2>
<p>...</p>A paragraph of text.<p>Welcome to my site.</p>
<br>A line break. No closing tag.Line one<br>Line two
<strong>...</strong> / <b>Bold. <strong> also tells screen readers the words are important.<strong>Note:</strong> read this
<em>...</em> / <i>Italic. <em> adds emphasis, like stressing a word when you speak.I <em>love</em> learning
<span>...</span>Wraps a few words inside a line so you can style them.I am <span class="hl">curious</span>
Links and media
<a href="...">...</a>A link ("anchor") to another page or site.<a href="about.html">About me</a>
<img src="..." alt="...">An image. alt describes it for people who cannot see it. No closing tag.<img src="me.jpg" alt="Photo of Briana">
<audio>...</audio>Plays sound, with controls people can click.<audio src="song.mp3" controls></audio>
<video>...</video>Plays a video, with controls people can click.<video src="demo.mp4" controls></video>
Lists
<ul>...</ul>A bulleted ("unordered") list.<ul><li>HTML</li></ul>
<ol>...</ol>A numbered ("ordered") list.<ol><li>Step one</li></ol>
<li>...</li>One item in a list. Goes inside <ul> or <ol>.<li>CSS</li>
Tables
<table>...</table>Wraps a table of rows and columns.<table>...</table>
<tr>...</tr>One row of the table.<tr><td>API</td></tr>
<th>...</th>A header cell, usually bold, labeling a column.<th>Word</th>
<td>...</td>A regular cell with data in it.<td>Flask</td>
Forms
<form>...</form>Wraps the parts of a form people fill in and send.<form>...</form>
<input>A one-line box for typing, or a checkbox. No closing tag.<input type="email">
<textarea>...</textarea>A bigger box for typing several lines.<textarea></textarea>
<button>...</button>A clickable button.<button>Send</button>
Layout
<header>...</header>The top section of a page, often the logo and title.<header>...</header>
<nav>...</nav>The menu of links for moving around the site.<nav><a href="#work">Work</a></nav>
<main>...</main>The main content of the page. Use it once per page.<main>...</main>
<section>...</section>Groups related content, like one chapter of a page.<section id="about">...</section>
<article>...</article>A piece that makes sense on its own, like a blog post or a project card.<article>...</article>
<footer>...</footer>The bottom section, often contact info and copyright.<footer>...</footer>
<div>...</div>A plain box for grouping things when no other tag fits.<div class="card">...</div>

Tip: the opening and closing tags must match. <h1>Title</h1> works; <h1>Title</h2> does not.
