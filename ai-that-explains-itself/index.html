Project 01 — A reading tool for everyday people who use AI.

AI That Explains Itself

AI answers arrive fluent, confident and complete. That polish makes them easy to trust and hard to question. This page shows you what's underneath: the claims, the confidence, the sources and the assumptions, so you can decide what to believe.

01 — How to read it

FourLenses

  1. A

    Claim

    A fact you could check: a number, a date, a name, a "this is how it works."

    Ask: Where does that come from?

  2. B

    Confidence

    How sure the wording sounds. Hedges ("usually") and absolutes ("always") both tell you something.

    Ask: How sure are you, and why?

  3. C

    Source

    Who the answer leans on. "Experts say" is not a source. A name you can look up is.

    Ask: Which source, exactly?

  4. D

    Assumption

    What the answer decided about you without asking: where you live, what you earn, your health.

    Ask: What are you assuming about me?

03 — Try your own

PasteAn Answer

What this reader can't do

It reads wording, not truth. It spots patterns in the language — numbers, hedges, absolutes, vague sources, assumptions about you. It can't tell you whether the answer is right. That part is still yours.

It uses simple word lists you can read in script.js, not another AI. It will miss things and flag things that are fine.

    04 — Make it explain itself

    AskBack
